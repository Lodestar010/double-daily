Space Jellyfish: The Glowing Animal a Rocket Left Behind + Firefly + Ethereum's Glamsterdam Upgrade

Tuesday, October 20, 2026 · Watch on YouTube

▶

Before sunrise on Tuesday, October 13, 2026, a SpaceX Falcon 9 lifted off from Cape Canaveral at 6:33 a.m. Eastern, and its exhaust plume — sunlit high in the sky while the ground below was still dark — spread into a glowing jellyfish visible for hundreds of miles up the East Coast. NASA photographed it from Cocoa Beach, and this week it is one of the most searched stories in America.

THE PAST. The phenomenon is nearly fifty years old in the record. In September 1977, residents of Petrozavodsk in the Soviet Union watched a glowing jellyfish hang over their city for more than ten minutes; researchers later traced it to that morning's Kosmos-955 launch from the secret Plesetsk cosmodrome. In December 2017 the same effect over Los Angeles stopped traffic and flooded a police non-emergency line, and it repeated over California in October 2018 with the SAOCOM 1A launch. The recipe never changes: a launch near sunrise or sunset, a dark ground, a sunlit plume, and exhaust spreading into ice crystals high in the sky.

THE PRESENT. The rocket was flying NASA's 35th commercial resupply mission. Its Dragon cargo ship carried more than 6,300 pounds to the International Space Station, including the final pair of roll-out solar arrays — to be installed on spacewalks later this fall — plus artificial-retina material, 3D heart tissue models, glass-formation and brain-organoid investigations. Dragon docked on its own on October 15, ahead of schedule, and its first-stage booster, flying for the eighth time, landed back at the Cape minutes after liftoff.

THE FUTURE — confirmed only. The final solar arrays go in this fall, Dragon comes home in mid-November with a splashdown off California, and more twilight launches are on the calendar — another predawn Falcon 9 was scheduled from the Cape this very morning. On the web3 watch list, Ethereum's Glamsterdam upgrade activates on the Hoodi test network on October 26; no mainnet date has been announced. Not financial advice.

Today's American company

Firefly Aerospace

Founded in Cedar Park, Texas in 2014 as Firefly Space Systems and rebuilt in 2017 as Firefly Aerospace. Firefly builds the Alpha small-launch rocket, which first reached orbit in October 2022, and the Blue Ghost lunar lander. On March 2, 2025, Blue Ghost touched down in the Moon's Mare Crisium and completed its full planned mission of about fourteen days — the first fully successful Moon landing by a commercial company — under a $101.5 million NASA Commercial Lunar Payload Services contract. Firefly listed on the Nasdaq in August 2025, and more Blue Ghost missions are under contract for 2026 and 2028. Company facts only: no prices, no predictions.
