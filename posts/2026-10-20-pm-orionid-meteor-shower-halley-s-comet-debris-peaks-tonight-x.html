Orionid Meteor Shower: Halley's Comet Debris Peaks Tonight + X-energy + All-In + Solana's Alpenglow

Tuesday, October 20, 2026 · Watch on YouTube

▶

Tomorrow night, dust left behind by the most famous comet in history rains through our sky. The Orionid meteor shower is active now and peaks the night of October 21–22.

THE PAST. The story starts with records, not telescopes: astronomers in China logged this comet as early as 240 B.C. In 1705 Edmond Halley saw that three famous comets were one and predicted its return — the prediction held on Christmas Day 1758. In 1986 a spacecraft armada met the comet, and ESA's Giotto photographed its dark core. The dust it sheds crosses Earth's path twice a year.

THE PRESENT. The October crossing is the Orionids, named for Orion. This year the bright Moon is the one catch: best viewing is before dawn, after the Moon sets, at ten to twenty meteors an hour under dark skies. A G1 geomagnetic storm watch also ran October 19–20.

THE FUTURE — confirmed only. A full moon follows on October 26, the sister Eta Aquariid shower arrives next May, and Halley's Comet — which turned for home in December 2023 — returns in 2061.
